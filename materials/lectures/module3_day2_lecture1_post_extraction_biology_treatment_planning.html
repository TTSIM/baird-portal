Core Concept

Starting from –10

The moment a tooth is extracted, irreversible biological changes begin. The clinician must understand this to make good decisions.

Key Insight: "You and I understand that the moment we take a tooth out, we're starting from the –5 or –10. We start from the basement."

  • Negative soft tissue changes begin immediately — gum recession, limited keratinized mucosa
  • Coronal shift of the mucogingival line occurs
  • The labial plate (mostly bundle bone) begins resorption
  • Without stimulation: progressive 3D bone volume loss follows

⚠ The Challenge: Most people can place an implant. The challenge is understanding the biology — bone management, GBR, soft tissue — that determines the final result.

Biological Foundation

The Bundle Bone Theory

What is Bundle Bone?

  • Specialised bone that makes up the coronal 5mm of the labial plate
  • Relies almost entirely on the periodontal ligament (PDL) for its blood supply
  • When a tooth is extracted, the PDL is destroyed
  • Without blood supply, bundle bone undergoes predictable resorption

Inevitable Consequence: Regardless of what you do, you WILL lose the coronal 5mm of the labial plate. This happens because the PDL bundles — which provided blood supply — are ruptured at extraction.

Clinical Implication: Because of this, when placing immediate implants, we do NOT follow the root position. The implant must be positioned slightly palatal to account for predictable labial bone loss.

Clinical Factors

Factors Affecting Post-Extraction Bone Loss

Patient-Related Factors

  • Phenotype: Thin biotype patients lose more bone than thick biotype. This is predictable and must be accounted for in planning.
  • Age: A young patient heals far better. An elderly patient's bone remodelling is slower and less predictable.
  • Healing Ability: Systemic conditions (diabetes, immunosuppression) affect the rate and quality of healing.
  • Healing Time: Leaving a socket to heal for 2 years without intervention results in significantly more bone loss than 6 months.

Site-Related Factors

  • Extraction Technique: Atraumatic extraction preserves labial and interseptal bone. Rough technique destroys it.
  • Pre-existing Conditions: Trauma to a tooth causes microcracks in bone — dissolution follows.
  • Acute Infection: Body spends ~6 weeks clearing the infection before bone healing begins. Effectively "6 weeks minus".
  • Apical Pathology Size: A 7mm apical lesion means 6–8 weeks of infection clearance before healing. At 12 weeks, only woven bone is present.

Key Principle: Chronic infection heals faster than acute infection. The size of the apical pathology matters — "12 weeks" is not always enough.

Treatment Planning

Timing Protocols

Every implant case falls into one of three timing categories. Each has different biological and clinical implications.

⚡
Immediate

Immediate Placement

  • Implant placed at time of extraction
  • Must have no acute infection
  • Requires adequate apical bone for stability
  • Labial gap always present — must graft
  • Need longer implant for primary stability
⏱
Early

Early Placement

  • 4–8 weeks after extraction
  • Allows soft tissue to heal first
  • Socket walls intact enough for stability
  • Some bundle bone already resorbed
  • Better access for GBR than immediate
📅
Delayed

Delayed Placement

  • 3+ months after extraction
  • Socket fully healed with mature bone
  • Bone volume already reduced
  • Implant placed in correct position
  • May need shorter implant (no socket depth)

Remember: All three timing protocols will need some form of bone management (GBR). The timing changes the approach — not whether you graft.

Digital Dentistry

Why Digital Planning?

In 2026, there is no excuse not to use digital planning for implant cases. The information it provides is invaluable.

Benefits of Digital Planning

  • Communicates treatment to the patient with visual clarity
  • Identifies vital structures to avoid (nerve, sinus floor)
  • Shows both limitations and possibilities of the case
  • Reveals the required implant depth from the gingival margin
  • Confirms prosthetic position before any surgery begins
  • Enables surgical guide fabrication for safe, accurate placement

Workflow: Digital wax (STL) merged with CBCT DICOM. The lab measures 3mm from gingival zenith → implant shoulder depth determined. Implant position and angulation planned to emerge through the cingulum.

No Intraoral Scanner?

  • Take an alginate impression
  • Lab casts a model and scans it
  • STL file generated at low cost
  • Merge with CBCT for full digital plan

Full Arch vs Sectional Scan

  • Always use full arch CBCT for digital planning
  • Sectional scan = only ~3 reference points for STL merge
  • Full arch = ~10 reference points → accurate merge
  • Sectional: insufficient accuracy for surgical guides

⚠ Medico-Legal Shift: 15 years ago — "Why did you take CBCT?" Today — "Why did you NOT take CBCT?"

Full Surgical Guide — Detail

Every drill and the implant are placed through the guide. Sleeves prevent over-drilling and control angulation precisely. Critical point: If the guide isn't perfect, "it's like putting the wrong postcode into Google Maps — it will take you in the wrong direction." Planning accuracy is paramount. Best for guided full-arch surgery and complex cases.

Surgical Technique

Flap Design Principles

Before any incision, five anatomical questions must be answered. The correct flap design follows from this assessment.

The 5 Pre-Flap Questions

  • What is the mesial bone level? (Favourable or not?)
  • What is the distal bone level? (Favourable or not?)
  • What is the labial bone status?
  • Who are my neighbours? (Adjacent teeth anatomy)
  • What phenotype is this patient?

Thick Phenotype + Favourable Defect: Go for a three-sided flap with golf-club incisions on each side. This provides excellent access while preserving papillae.

The Three-Sided Flap (Golf Club)

  • Vertical relieving incisions placed mesial and distal
  • Each vertical incision has a curved "golf club" base
  • Protects blood supply to the flap edge
  • Allows full-thickness elevation for good visibility
  • Can be extended for GBR access if needed

Press Release Technique

  • Blade placed parallel to flap pointing toward bone, ~5° upright
  • One 1mm notch in the flap base
  • Notch expands to 10mm+ when the flap is stretched
  • Allows tension-free primary closure after GBR
  • Must be done every time GBR is performed
Surgical Principle

Implant Positioning in the Socket

For immediate implants, the position within the socket is critical. Following the root position is a common and serious error.

⚠ Never follow the root position: Bundle bone on the labial will resorb. An implant placed at the root position will become exposed as this bone disappears.

Correct Positioning Rules

  • Shift the implant as little as possible within the socket — palatally/lingually
  • Do NOT invade or damage the palatal bone — just gently touch it
  • Aim to emerge through the cingulum region
  • This creates a labial gap (jumping gap) which must be grafted
  • The gap provides a biological buffer for bundle bone resorption

The Jumping Gap: The space between the implant labial surface and the socket wall. Always fill with bone graft — it protects against inevitable bundle bone resorption.

Primary Stability

Torque & Primary Stability

Primary stability (insertion torque in Ncm) is the key determinant of the loading protocol chosen at the time of surgery.

How is Torque Measured?

  • The implant motor provides feedback — but is only accurate if regularly calibrated
  • For loading decisions, always use the torque wrench for accuracy
  • Apply the carrier and tighten with the torque wrench
  • If it hits 35Ncm and the implant does not move → safe to load

What Affects Torque?

  • Implant thread design — aggressive vs regular threads
  • Gap between osteotomy diameter and implant diameter (underpreparing)
  • Bone density — D1/D2 achieves high torque; D4 makes immediate loading very difficult

Clinical Protocol

Torque Thresholds — Decision Framework

These thresholds guide the surgical decision at implant placement. They are not absolute — additional clinical factors must always be considered.

Torque Achieved Protocol Clinical Action Notes
≥ 35 Ncm Immediate Loading Fit temporary crown chairside. Implant can be loaded immediately. Also need: no parafunctional habits, provisional ready, patient-appropriate risk profile
25–34 Ncm Transmucosal Place long healing abutment. Suture around it. No immediate loading. One-stage protocol. Monitor for 8–12 weeks.
Below 25 Ncm Submerge (Two-Stage) Bury the implant. Use short healing cap. Suture fully over it. Protect implant from micromotion at all costs. Return for Stage 2 after osseointegration.

⚠ Important: "I can promise a patient immediate placement — but I can NEVER promise immediate loading." Always prepare a contingency (e.g. a hollow Rochette) before surgery in case torque is insufficient.

Tip: For immediate loading goals — use aggressive/deep threads, underprepare the osteotomy (e.g. use 3.3mm drill for a 4.1mm implant), and choose denser bone sites.

Two-Stage Protocol Timelines

Mandible 8 weeks to osseointegration
Maxilla 12 weeks to osseointegration

Note: If movement is detected when checking the implant at stage 2 — STOP. Go clockwise first to confirm integration. Movement may indicate woven bone, not failure. Leave for a further 20 weeks before re-assessing.

Prosthodontic Management

Temporary Crown & Soft Tissue Sculpting

The temporary crown is not just a space-holder — it is an active tool for shaping the peri-implant soft tissue architecture.

Healing Abutment vs Temporary Abutment

  • Healing abutment: Creates a circular emergence profile. Adequate but not ideal for aesthetics.
  • Temporary abutment: Has a chimney and grooves. Can be cut and shaped to any height. Composite or pMMA crown built onto it.
  • Temporary abutments come in different heights — cut to what you need

Progressive Tissue Sculpting

  • Add flowable composite every 2 weeks
  • Gradually contour toward the final emergence profile
  • Push the zenith to match the neighbouring tooth incrementally
  • Gradual pressure = tissue displacement not necrosis
  • One-step convex profile = pressure = recession

Critical Rule — Subgingival Profile: The subgingival portion of the temporary crown must be concave. A convex profile pushes bone and soft tissue away → recession.

Digital Impression: Use a scan body (fully seated, confirmed by X-ray). Lab builds screw-retained crown with access hole at the cingulum.

Implant Selection

Implant Sizing — Diameter

Diameter selection is driven by mechanics, biology, and the restorative space available — not just bone width.

Diameter Selection Principles

  • Anterior aesthetics: 3.7mm preferred for lateral incisors — preserves inter-implant space for papilla
  • Cantilever cases: Avoid narrow connection (NC) — increased lateral forces may cause fracture
  • Implant-to-implant papilla: More space between implants = better papilla height
  • Buccal bone: "Easy to make it fatter" — can always graft buccally
  • Mesiodistal available bone is the true limiting factor

Rule of Thumb: For a cantilever bridge replacing laterals, choose 4.1mm (RC) rather than 3.7mm (NC). Consider 3.7mm mesial + 4.1mm distal to maximise inter-implant space for papilla support.

Implant Design

Platform Switching

Discovered accidentally — and now a standard feature in all modern implants. Understanding why it matters requires understanding bacterial microleakage.

The Story of Platform Switching

  • A company launched 5mm implants but had no 5mm abutments ready
  • Frustrated clinicians placed 4mm abutments on 5mm implants
  • Later discovered: those cases had significantly BETTER bone levels
  • Investigation revealed the mismatch moved the bacterial leakage point away from the bone

The House Analogy: Bacterial leakage = a noisy student house next door. Platform switching moves you 3 houses away — the noise (bacteria) arrives diluted. But better still: enforce quiet hours — make the connection RIGID so little bacteria enters in the first place.

Modern Priority: Connection RIGIDITY is more important than platform switching degree. A rigid connection with minimal platform switching outperforms a leaky connection with large platform switching (e.g. Ankylos — biggest platform switching, but narrow abutment = fracture risk + rough subcrestal surface = bacteria trap).

Stage 2 Surgery

Implant Exposure Techniques

When a submerged (two-stage) protocol is used, the second surgery exposes the implant. This is also an opportunity to augment soft tissue.

Checking Integration First

  • Before going anticlockwise, first try CLOCKWISE on the healing cap
  • If no movement clockwise → integrated. Safe to proceed.
  • If rotational movement exists but no axial movement → woven bone stage. Leave for 20 more weeks.
  • Axial movement = fibrous healing. Remove the implant.

Exposure Options

  • Punch technique: Only appropriate if adequate keratinized mucosa is present buccally
  • H-incision: Redistributes keratinized mucosa from the crest to buccal AND lingual. Especially valuable in the mandible.
  • Roll flap: Rolls palatal connective tissue inwards to increase buccal soft tissue thickness

Roll Flap Technique

  • De-epithelialise the palatal mucosa (0.3mm epithelium) using high-speed or blade
  • Roll the connective tissue inward toward the buccal aspect
  • Creates double-thickness soft tissue on the buccal
  • Can add synthetic soft tissue graft for further augmentation
  • Results in excellent emergence profile volume

Crown Fitting After Contouring: When the final crown is fitted, blanching of the gingiva is acceptable for the first 5 minutes. If blanching does not resolve → the gingival profile is too bulky → send back to lab to under-contour. Leaving it will cause recession.

Clinical Case

Case Study: Immediate Placement, Immediate Loading

A retained deciduous tooth with a 3-week surgical deadline — demonstrating how biology and clinical decision making come together.

Presentation — August 2012

Thomas (teacher), retained upper lateral deciduous tooth — wobbly. Starting first job in September: no time off until February.

Decision: Immediate Placement

Delayed placement not an option. Longer narrow implant placed immediately. Osteotomy under-prepared to maximise primary stability.

Primary Stability: 35 Ncm achieved

Torque wrench confirmed ≥35Ncm → immediate loading possible. Chairside temporary crown fabricated (pMMA). Subgingival profile: concave.

GBR for Convexity

Xenograft placed to build convexity. Membrane applied and sutured. Patient leaves to begin his career with a functional temporary crown.

12 Weeks: Final Impression

Back in 2012: cement-retained final crown (screw-retained not yet standard). Zirconia abutment + crown fitted. Contralateral veneer for symmetry.

14 Years Post-Op (2026)

Remarkable soft tissue stability. Crown updated, composite bonding added. Demonstrates that correct subgingival emergence profile = long-term success.

Key Learning: A longer implant was chosen specifically because of the 3-week deadline (needed high torque for immediate loading). With no time pressure, an 8–10mm implant without under-preparation would have been appropriate. Always match implant strategy to the clinical reality.

Overview

The Four Types of Bone Grafting

Every bone grafting decision in implant dentistry falls into one of these four categories. Understanding the distinction is fundamental.

🪥

1. Socket Grafting

Grafting the socket immediately after extraction to preserve ridge volume. Covered in detail by Victoria (Sunday morning). Material choice depends on planned re-entry timing.

🔄

2. Guided Bone Regeneration (GBR)

Simultaneous with implant placement or shortly after. Uses bone particles + membrane. The "bread and butter" of implant dentistry. Covered in Lecture 2.

🏗

3. Staged Bone Grafting

Block graft, sausage technique, or bone plate. Performed BEFORE implant placement. Required when the defect has insufficient walls for GBR. Covered in Lecture 3.

🌬

4. Sinus Grafting

Internal or external. Required when maxillary sinus has pneumatized and insufficient bone height exists for implant placement. Covered in Lecture 3.

Decision Making

Simultaneous GBR vs Staged Approach

The most important clinical decision in bone grafting — knowing WHEN you can regenerate simultaneously versus when you must stage.

The Three Questions

  • Can I place the implant in the correct prosthetic position?
  • Can I achieve adequate primary stability?
  • Is the resulting defect favourable (≥2 bony walls remaining)?

If YES to all three: Simultaneous GBR. Place the implant and graft at the same time.

If NO to any one: Staged approach. Build the bone first, then return (typically 24 weeks) to place the implant.

Defect Wall Classification

  • 3-wall defect: Mesial + distal + one labial wall. Highly favourable for GBR.
  • 2-wall defect: Mesial + distal peaks. Often still favourable if some stability exists.
  • 1-wall or no-wall defect: Insufficient stability for bone particles. No blood supply scaffold. Must stage.
  • Knife-edge defect: No distal wall. Bone particles cannot be contained. Must stage.

GBR Bridge Analogy: "You cannot build a bridge between a mountain and infinity — but you can build it between two mountains." Bony walls ARE the mountains that support your regeneration.

Clinical Philosophy

Implant Treatment as a Customised Journey

Every patient's treatment plan is unique. There are always stages, and these take time. Patients must understand this from the outset.

The Four Stages

  • Diagnosis: All cases should receive the same rigorous diagnostic process
  • Chosen Outcome: The most important stage — determines everything that follows
  • Treatment Technique: Different techniques can achieve the same outcome — no "one right way"
  • Review: Long-term follow-up and maintenance

Key Message: "You do not sell dental implants. A dental implant is a treatment journey — customised for each patient. You cannot quote a flat fee."

The Fast / Good / Cheap Triangle

Clinical Case

Case Study: Trauma — Staged Block Graft

Young woman, night out, fell and traumatised a central incisor. Entire tooth and bone destroyed. Demonstrates the staged approach when GBR is insufficient.

Presentation

Central incisor and surrounding bone completely destroyed. Insufficient bone to place implant in correct prosthetic position — implant would "float in the air".

Decision: Staged Block Graft

Simultaneous GBR not possible — no wall defect. Block bone sourced from human tissue bank (allograft block). Avoids second donor site for patient.

Block Placement — Week 0

Block shaped and contoured to fit. Secured and left to integrate. Timeline: went from "–10" to "+1". Did NOT aim for "–10 to +10 in one surgery".

Now sufficient bone for correct prosthetic implant placement. Prosthetic guide used to confirm position. Implant placed + simultaneous GBR for convexity.

Palatal roll flap + synthetic soft tissue augmentation. Progressive temporary crown sculpting over 8–10 weeks (every 2 weeks).

Compared to Victoria's case (24 weeks) — staged approach takes longer but was the only biologically correct option. Patient had concave temporary throughout.

Bridge Design

Pontic Site Grafting

Bone grafting is not only for sites receiving implants. Pontic sites must also be grafted to achieve ideal restorative outcomes.

Why Graft the Pontic?

  • Without grafting, the pontic site loses volume → concavity
  • Concavity forces the lab to create a ridge-lap design
  • Ridge-lap traps food, plaque, bacteria — impossible to clean
  • Even if it looks good today, continual remodelling causes progressive recession under the bridge
  • Grafting allows an ovate pontic design → hygienic and aesthetic

⚠ Case Learning: "I placed two implants and grafted beautifully — but forgot to graft the central pontic. I ended up with a ridge lap there. My mistake of imperfect sausage graft accidentally grafted the lateral pontic, but the central was missed."

Message for All Implantologists: "Bone grafting improves or ruins the quality of the prosthesis above." You cannot restore well on top of poor bone management.

Posterior Ridge Contouring

  • Upper posterior: buccal cusp must deflect cheek — needs width
  • Without adequate ridge width → narrow crown → patient bites cheek, OR ridge-lap, OR large undercut
  • GBR creates the wide base for hygienic emergence
  • Lower posterior: lingual cusp must NOT be moved inward — tongue injury risk
Biomechanics

Implant Angle & Peri-implantitis Risk

Where the implant is placed within the ridge has a direct and measurable impact on long-term peri-implant health.

The Angle Problem

  • Narrow ridge → clinicians often place the implant in the centre of the narrow ridge
  • Centre of a narrow ridge = LINGUAL position for the implant shoulder
  • This creates a large angle between implant axis and crown axis
  • Studies show: the greater the offset angle → higher risk of peri-implantitis
  • Ideal: implant placed in the centre of the PROSTHETIC envelope, not the ridge centre

Clinical Comparison

ScenarioImplant PositionResult
Narrow ridge, no graftingLingual (ridge centre)90° angle → peri-implantitis risk
Grafted ridge (GBR)Prosthetic centre<40° angle → stable

Solution: Do NOT place an implant in the middle of the narrow ridge and accept the resulting angulation. Create the bone with GBR, THEN place the implant in the correct prosthetic position. The bone regeneration serves the restoration — not the other way around.

Clinical Understanding

Three Levels of GBR Understanding

Most clinicians operate at Level 1. The goal is to reach Level 3. Understanding this hierarchy helps you differentiate yourself clinically.

01

Level 1 — ~90% of Implantologists

"I graft when I see exposed threads." No exposed threads = no GBR. This is the lowest level of understanding — ignores the critical 2mm rule and long-term stability.

02

Level 2 — ~50% of Implantologists

"I graft to achieve 2mm buccal bone AND build convexity, especially anterior." Understands aesthetics. Still misses posterior implications.

03

Level 3 — ~25% of Implantologists

"I graft every case — anterior AND posterior — to build a wide, hygienic platform with correct emergence. My prosthetics team can design without compromise." Grafts pontic sites too.

ITI Consensus (Sept 2023): Thick buccal bone = stable outcome. Thin buccal bone + no GBR = predictable thread exposure and peri-implantitis. Thin buccal bone + correct GBR = predictable long-term protection.

Case Comparison

GBR vs Staged: Comparing Two Journeys

Both cases achieve the same goal — a beautiful, stable anterior implant. The path depends entirely on the defect at presentation.

Victoria's Case — 24 Weeks
  • Immediate or early placement possible
  • Sufficient primary stability
  • Favourable 3-wall defect
  • Simultaneous GBR at implant placement
  • 12 weeks submerged → Stage 2 exposure
  • Progressive temporary contouring → final crown

Short, predictable, biology-respecting. The ideal scenario when anatomy allows.

Block Graft Case — 18 Months
  • Severely deficient bone — no wall defect
  • GBR not possible — no stability, no scaffold
  • Human tissue bank block placed first
  • 24 weeks for block integration

Longer, more complex — but the only biologically correct route for this defect. Patient had aesthetic temporary throughout.

The One Non-Negotiable: Regardless of which path — the implant must be in the correct prosthetic position. Everything else — the grafting, the timing, the technique — serves this single goal.
