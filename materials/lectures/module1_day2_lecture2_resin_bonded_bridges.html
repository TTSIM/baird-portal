Resin Bonded Bridges

From transitional prostheses to long-term solutions — design, cementation & case selection

Arrow keys or swipe to navigate

Context

Why Learn Resin Bonded Bridges?

Young Patients

Implants in growing patients become infra-occluded (ankylosed, no PDL). Wait until growth is complete: ~21 for males, up to 25 for females.

During Implant Journey

Fixed interim prosthesis that minimises pressure on healing bone and soft tissue. Better than a denture for patients who need something fixed.

Medically Compromised

Patients unsuitable for implant surgery: uncontrolled diabetes, radiotherapy >50Gy, IV bisphosphonates, high ASA status, reduced mental capacity.

Strategy: RBB as Plan A. If it fails → the patient now understands implants may be the only fixed option. If it works → great, money saved.

Overview

Advantages & Disadvantages

Advantages ✓

  • Fixed replacement
  • Tooth-supported → minimal resorption
  • Conservative (technically reversible)
  • No local anaesthesia needed
  • Only 2 visits, minimal chair time
  • 80% survival at 4–10 years
  • Not very technique-sensitive
VS

Disadvantages ✗

  • Best for single-tooth spans
  • Requires good enamel for bonding
  • Relies on adhesive retention (can debond)
  • Risk of silent debond (fixed-fixed)
  • Framework fracture risk
  • Aesthetic considerations (metal shine-through)

Always make a backup Essex retainer with teeth/pontic paint in case the bridge debonds at a weekend.

EVIDENCE SUMMARY

  • Cantilever design is better than fixed-fixed
  • No or minimal prep is better than prepped
  • No contact on the retainer in excursions
  • Unrestored abutment teeth perform better than restored ones
  • Only recommended prep: minimal guide plane preparation
Design

Choosing Your Abutment Tooth

Six factors determine which adjacent tooth makes the best abutment:

1. Enamel availability

Maximum enamel = maximum bond strength. Avoid composite-covered palatal surfaces.

2. Endodontic status

Root-treated teeth reduce bridge survival. Avoid if possible.

3. Root length

Minimum 1:1 crown-to-root ratio for the abutment.

4. Surface area

More palatal surface area = more retention. Central incisors often have more area than canines.

5. Connector height

The connector must be tall enough to prevent fracture. This often determines the abutment choice.

6. Resistance form

Cingulum provides resistance form. Flat teeth = less retention.

Also consider: translucency (metal shine-through risk), previous orthodontics (relapse risk).

Critical Dimension

Connector Height

The connector is the weakest point. If it's too short, it fractures.

MINIMUM CONNECTOR DIMENSIONS

Metal

2mm × 2mm (4mm²). Can use canines even with shorter connector height.

Zirconia

4–5mm height × 2mm width (~9–11mm²). Requires taller connectors → may dictate abutment choice.

Tip: You can increase connector height by preparing a minimal guide plane — reducing the bulbosity between the pontic and abutment allows a taller connector without altering the occlusion.

Lab Prescription

Bridge Design Specifications

WHAT TO TELL YOUR LAB

Retainer wing: ≥0.7mm (metal) or ≥0.5mm (zirconia)

Connector: 2×2mm (metal) or ~11mm² (zirconia)

Wrap-around: 180° full retainer extension

Locating lug: Metal retainers — include a lug/dimple for precise seating (cut off after cementation)

Occlusion: No contact on retainer in excursions

Pontic: Ovate (ideal) or modified ridge lap. Never a saddle design.

PONTIC DESIGN COMPARISON

Ovate pontics create the most natural emergence profile. Modified ridge lap is the minimum acceptable — buccal contact, palatal cut-back.

Strategy

Transitional vs Long-Term Design

Transitional (Implant Journey)

  • Fixed-fixed design (stays better short-term)
  • Metal with perforated retainer (Rochette)
  • Cemented with resin-modified GIC (easy removal)
  • Removal: scaler through holes → crown removal tool → tap off
  • Always wrap floss around the bridge first
VS

Long-Term (Definitive)

  • Cantilever design (better survival)
  • Zirconia or metal retainer
  • Cemented with resin cement (Panavia)
  • Consider connector dimensions carefully
  • Full 180° wrap-around
Cementation

Cementing Metal Bridges (Panavia)

STEP-BY-STEP PROTOCOL

1. Sandblast the retainer ▾

Aluminium oxide particles. Creates micro-mechanical retention.

2. Wash and dry ▾

Remove all debris from sandblasting.

3. Apply alloy primer (optional for CoCr) ▾

Strictly needed for precious metals. For non-precious CoCr, optional but doesn't hurt.

4. Apply gel on tooth → 30 sec → air dry ▾

Do NOT light cure. Gently air dry only.

5. Mix cement, apply, seat bridge ▾

Panavia F2: flash cure. Panavia 21: wipe excess with microbrush.

6. Apply Oxyguard ▾

Inhibits the oxygen layer for complete cure.

Key point: Cement WITHOUT rubber dam for resin bonded bridges — it gets in the way. Moisture control with cotton rolls and suction is sufficient.

After applying the tooth surface gel for 30 seconds, what do you do next?

Cementation

Bonding Zirconia: The APC Concept

Marcus Blatz's APC concept makes zirconia bridges viable. Success comparable to metal.

A

Air particle Abrasion

Sandblast wings with 30-micron particles at low pressure. Creates micro-mechanical retention.

P

Primer (MDP)

Apply zirconia-specific primer with MDP (e.g. MKZ primer, Bredent). Replaces silane in the standard protocol.

C

Cement (Resin)

Resin-based cement (e.g. Calibra). Swap the silane for the MDP zirconia primer. Rest of the protocol stays the same.

Zirconia selection: Anterior bridges → minimum 4Y zirconia (stronger, slightly more opaque). Posterior → 3Y zirconia (strongest). 5Y zirconia is more translucent but only suitable for single crowns — too weak for bridge connectors.

Case Study

Zirconia Bridges: Replacing Metal Retainers

PROBLEMS IDENTIFIED

  • Metal shine-through on translucent central incisors
  • Pontics too short (gingival margin 2–3mm short vs ideal 1–2mm)
  • Shade mismatch (opaque pontics)
  • Ridge-lapped pontic design

TREATMENT SEQUENCE

  • 1. Remove old bridges
  • 2. Fit immediate partial denture
  • 3. Electrosurgery to allow taller pontics
  • 4. Condition soft tissues (2 weeks)
  • 5. Whitening
  • 6. Take impressions/scans
  • 7. Cement zirconia bridges (APC concept)

RESULT

No metal shine-through, correct pontic dimensions, natural shade match, comparable to implant result. Patient was young — can progress to implants when ready.

Case Study

Natural Tooth Pontic Bridge

73-year-old lady with external cervical root resorption — hopeless prognosis. Characterful teeth impossible to match with conventional methods. On Apixaban.

TECHNIQUE

  • Metal retainer with locating lugs on both adjacent teeth
  • Extract tooth
  • Clean root canal, place composite, reshape cervical area to ovate bullet shape
  • Bond tooth to metal framework outside the mouth
  • Collagen sponge + PTFE tape in socket
  • Cement with resin-modified GIC
  • Cut lugs off, polish
  • Sutures (patient on blood thinners)

OUTCOME: 3+ YEARS AND COUNTING

Expected lifespan was 3–6 months. Excellent soft tissue healing thanks to ovate pontic shape and well-polished composite. Patient wrote a grateful letter 9 days post-op.

WHY IT WORKED

  • Perfect colour match — it's the patient's actual tooth
  • Ovate pontic shape guided soft tissue healing
  • Composite repair in cervical area well-polished
  • Bought time for implant planning and saving

Modern improvement: use a 3D-printed model without the tooth as a jig for bonding. Clean resorption area with degranulation burs (Meisinger).

Complications

Post-Op & Complications

COMPLICATION MANAGEMENT

Debond

Click to flip

Most common complication. Not catastrophic — can be recemented. Keep the bridge, reschedule patient.

Framework Fracture

Click to flip

Requires remake or alternative treatment. Usually due to inadequate connector dimensions or bruxism.

Silent Debond (Fixed-Fixed)

Click to flip

One wing debonds while the other holds — caries develops underneath undetected. Check regularly during implant journey.

CLEANING INSTRUCTIONS

Super Floss

Thread the stiff end under the pontic, pull through. The spongy section cleans the fitting surface. Essential for all bridge patients.

Interdental Brushes

If space allows, use to clean around the retainer wings.

Emergency Backup

Always provide an Essex retainer with pontic as backup. Weekend debonds happen — give patients a solution.

Summary

Key Takeaways

  • Cantilever design outperforms fixed-fixed for long-term bridges
  • Don't prep — evidence shows non-prepped has higher success
  • Choose abutments based on enamel, surface area, and connector height
  • Metal: 2×2mm connectors, Panavia cement
  • Zirconia: APC concept (Air abrasion, Primer with MDP, resin Cement)
  • Natural tooth pontics can buy years of time
  • Always provide a backup Essex retainer
