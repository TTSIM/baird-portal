Paradigm Shift

Short Implants: The Evidence

The old thinking was "longer = better." The evidence says otherwise.

70% of short implant failures occurred BEFORE loading — suggesting failures were surgical (poor primary stability, over-prepping), not biomechanical.

Cochrane review (Esposito): Short implants in 4–6mm bone below the sinus work without augmentation. Less cost, less trauma, comparable results.

Key insight: 95% of occlusal load is on the coronal 5mm. Going from 8mm to 19mm only reduces peri-implant stress by 6–8%. Not worth the risk.

Length Selection

The Plan A / Plan B Philosophy

Plan A: 8mm (Healed Sites)

As good as 9, 10, 12, or 15mm. Standard choice. Maximises success without unnecessary drilling depth, heat generation, or proximity to vital structures.

Anatomical Constraints: 6–7mm

ID nerve or sinus proximity. Slightly lower success rate but still viable. Consider splinting two implants together.

Plan B Backup: 10–12mm

Reserve longer implants for backup if you over-prep and need more stability. Never use your longest option first.

Ultra-Short: <6mm

Possible but more biomechanical considerations. Below 5mm of bone: consider sinus lift.

INTERACTIVE: Implant Length Decision

Available bone: 8mm

✓ 8mm — Plan A. Ideal length for a healed site.

Why 8mm? Increasing from 8 to 19mm only reduces stress by 6–8%. Not worth the drilling depth, heat risk, and proximity to nerves/sinuses.

Biomechanics

Crown Height Matters More Than Crown:Root Ratio

Crown-to-root ratio doesn't cause implant fracture. But crown HEIGHT drives prosthetic complications.

≥15mm crown height = higher risk of screw loosening, screw fracture, crown fracture. This is the threshold.

A longer implant (sinus lift) does NOT help if the bone level and therefore the crown height remain unchanged. The "lamppost" is still the same height.

Solutions: Vertical bone augmentation (reduces crown height), or splint multiple implants together (shares the load).

The T Concept

How Deep to Place Your Implant

The most important concept: place the implant 3–4mm apical to the future gingival margin (zenith).

THE TWO RULES

Rule 1: Biological Width

Mucosa needs 3–4mm of undisturbed space. Bone remodels to the first micro-rough surface (implant platform). Polished surfaces (healing abutments) cause bone to recede.

Rule 2: Mesial & Distal Peaks

Check if mesial and distal bone peaks exist at the T-line level. If yes → graft buccal/lingual between them. If not → go deeper and accept longer crown, or do staged bone grafting.

Thick phenotype → 4mm. Thin phenotype → 3mm. The thicker the mucosa, the more resistance to recession.

Position

Bucco-Lingual: Follow the Prosthetic Envelope

ITI guidelines: implant shoulder 2mm palatal to the future labial line (level with the cingulum of the adjacent tooth).

TOO LABIAL

Puts pressure on the cortical plate → it disappears → mid-labial recession. The "primary school teacher" scenario.

TOO PALATAL

Ridge-lap crown, phonetics issues, harder to clean. Avoid the "palatal approach" trend — peri-implantitis doesn't differentiate between buccal and palatal exposed threads.

GOLDEN RULE

Never compromise implant position. If bone is insufficient, graft it. Don't chase primary stability at the expense of the prosthetic envelope.

The 4th Dimension

Angulation: Two Schools of Thought

School 1: Chase Primary Stability

Angle implant into solid palatal bone for maximum torque. Sleep well tonight — but compromise the prosthetic envelope.

  • High primary stability today
  • Outside prosthetic envelope
  • Apex pushes into labial plate
  • Cement-retained or angled screw needed
  • More prosthetic complications

School 2: Follow the Prosthetic Envelope ✓

Place within the prosthetic envelope even if primary stability is lower. Easy to restore in 12–24 weeks.

  • Correct position for the restoration
  • Naturally further from labial plate (bonus!)
  • Screw-retained access possible
  • If apical fenestration → GBR
  • Better long-term biology

The most important conclusion: Follow the prosthetic envelope. Never compromise position for stability.

Limitations

Angled Correction Screws: Not a Free Pass

Angled screws seem like a solution for mal-positioned implants, but they come with trade-offs.

Extra cost: £75+ lab fee per unit

Lower PES: Poor implant position → thin bone/mucosa → reduced pink aesthetics (7.9 vs 9.0)

Larger access hole: Oval not round → bigger composite filling → more likely to debond

More screw loosening: Ball-in-socket instead of hex-in-hex → slipping → less true torque

C-shape abutment: Not a full circle → less retention → crown can debond

5 FACTORS CONTROLLING PAPILLA

1. Soft tissue thickness — thicker = better. Use connective tissue grafts.

2. Bone peak stability — controlled by neighbouring tooth health or inter-implant distance.

3. Contact point — no contact = no papilla. Diastema = papilla will never grow.

4. Clean embrasure — rough surfaces or poor hygiene prevents growth.

5. Blood supply — smokers and diabetics get black triangles. Period.

Biology

The Micro-Gap: Why Connection Design Matters

The implant-abutment junction creates a micro-gap that allows bacterial ingress. This is the main driver of early bone loss.

"What really matters is how rigid and tight my seal is." You either have a leaking implant or a non-leaking implant. Osteointegration is easy — the real challenge is zero bone loss after years.

IMPLICATIONS

  • Use premium implant systems with tight connections
  • Two implants close together → inflammatory zones overlap → more vertical bone loss
  • Wider spacing (≥3mm between implants) prevents overlap
  • This is why distance rules exist — not just blood supply, but bacterial leakage too
Restorative

Cantilever Rules in Implant Dentistry

5 RULES FOR CANTILEVERED IMPLANT CROWNS

1. Regular connection only (4.1, 4.4, 4.8mm) — no narrow

2. Pontic must be SMALLER than abutment tooth (e.g. lateral off central ✓, not central off lateral ✗)

3. Posterior: pontic should be MESIAL (forces increase distally)

4. Reduced occlusion on the pontic

5. Anti-rotational feature on the abutment base

2 IMPLANTS + 3 UNIT BRIDGE: THE DEFAULT

Study of 145 three-unit prostheses: two implants with a bridge had 100% survival, lowest peri-implantitis, and fewest prosthetic complications. Three implants with non-splinted crowns had the worst outcomes.

Safety

CBCT: What to Look For

CBCT is standard protocol. You'll never regret taking one — you will regret not taking one if things go wrong.

CHECKLIST FOR EVERY CBCT

1. Nerve position — ID nerve, mental foramen. ≥2mm safety (UK trend: 4mm).

2. Bone width — enough for implant + 1.8mm buccal + 1.5mm lingual?

3. Bone height — distance to sinus floor or ID nerve.

4. Bone density — white (dense) vs grey (medium) vs black (soft/void). Anticipate where you'll feel resistance vs drop.

5. Lingual undercut & arteries — sublingual (branch of lingual) above mylohyoid. Submental (branch of facial) below mylohyoid.

6. Adjacent roots — proximity, dilaceration, path of root canal.

ARTERIAL DANGER

Hitting an artery can be fatal. Arteries have elastic fibres — when cut, they shrink and retract, making the bleeding source impossible to locate. Blood outside vessels triggers foreign body response → massive swelling → potential airway obstruction.

WHY 2mm ISN'T ENOUGH

Human error (hand can slip). Vibration travels through cancellous bone. Implant placement crushes bone — pressure/bleeding can affect the nerve even without direct contact. 4mm safety zone is the emerging UK standard.

Technique

Internal Sinus Lift

A minimally invasive alternative to the lateral window sinus lift. Done through the osteotomy hole simultaneously with implant placement.

KEY POINTS

  • Special burs cut anti-clockwise
  • Irrigation pressure lifts the Schneiderian membrane
  • No extra incisions needed
  • Better healing than lateral window approach
  • Done simultaneously with implant placement
  • Use aggressive threads (e.g. Straumann BLX) in soft maxillary bone

When to use: Need ≥6mm implant but have <6mm of bone below the sinus. The internal lift gets you the extra 2–3mm. Much less invasive than a lateral window approach.

Still follow all protocols: place platform 3–4mm below planned gingival zenith. Don't place too high just to avoid the sinus.
