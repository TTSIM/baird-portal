Clinical Reality

Creating the Illusion of Nature

The soft tissue around a dental implant looks identical to natural tissue — but it is biologically different.

Bone Thickness Thresholds

  • Buccal bone: unless ≥2 mm, the mucosa will not stay stable
  • Soft tissue: unless ≥2 mm thick, it will not remain
  • 1 mm acceptable in some areas, but 2–4 mm is optimal

"Our patients expect us to replicate nature. We need to work hard to give them this illusion — and with intelligent technique, we can."

Critical dimensional thresholds for long-term stability

Why It Matters

Bone Grafting is No Longer Optional

Optimal bone grafting is not a bonus skill — it is an essential competency for every implant dentist.

"You cannot separate implant placement from bone grafting and soft tissue management. An integrated approach from day one is the only ethical way to treat patients."

Factors Affecting Bone Loss After Extraction

  • Extraction technique — atraumatic vs forceful removal changes buccal plate integrity
  • Healing time — early remodelling is most aggressive in the first 3–6 months
  • Patient healing ability — systemic factors, diabetes, medications, smoking
  • Trauma and infection — pre-existing pathology accelerates vertical/horizontal collapse
  • Phenotype — thin biotype patients lose more and faster

The alveolus can lose up to 50% of its width in the first year and 40% of its height over 3 years without intervention.

Historical Context

The Era of Autogenous Bone Blocks

1980s–2010s
Autogenous Block — Gold Standard Era
Harvested from chin (symphysis), ascending ramus, or body of mandible. Fixed with titanium or stainless steel screws. Considered the gold standard for horizontal and vertical augmentation.
2012
Shift to Allograft Blocks
Recognition that autogenous trauma was unacceptable for private practice patients. Human tissue bank allografts became commercially viable.
~2018
CAD-CAM Customised Blocks
Digital planning + human tissue banks began milling patient-specific blocks. Surgery time reduced from 45 minutes to 45 seconds for block placement.
2022–Present
Magnesium Membrane Era
Resorbable rigid magnesium plates replace PTFE. Dissolves into magnesium salts that actively improve bone quality. Bacteriostatic when exposed.
Autogenous Blocks

Advantages of Autogenous

  • Gold standard biology — own bone, no rejection risk
  • Mechanical stability — cancellous + cortical structure
  • Volume stability — predictable 3D augmentation
  • Proven literature — decades of evidence
  • No cost for material itself

"If your patient is in pain, what do you do? In hospital: morphine pump. In private practice: ibuprofen 600mg. In private practice we should have a different mentality."

spacer

The Complications

  • Two surgical sites — donor + recipient site trauma
  • Extended surgery time — harvesting, shaping, adapting
  • Swelling — "double black eyes, swollen like hamsters"
  • Risk of block loss — dropped/cracked block = start over
  • Nerve damage risk at donor site (chin, ramus)
  • Neighbour tooth injury — direct or via blood supply
  • Altered sensation in donor area
  • Infection/exposure at donor site

If the block is dropped, cracked by a screw, or lost: you must harvest a new block — adding 90+ minutes to surgery.

Allograft Blocks

The Allograft Advantage

Human tissue bank allograft blocks offer the same 3D augmentation capability with dramatically reduced patient trauma.

  • No second surgical site — eliminates donor trauma
  • Saves ~50% of surgery time — no harvesting phase
  • Ready-made — still requires shaping/contouring, but no drilling through chin
  • Replaceable — if dropped or cracked, open another block
  • Always keep a spare block — costs ~£300 but saves the case
  • Better patient experience — simpler post-operative recovery

"If this happens to your allograft block — Next, open another block. Yes, you've just lost £300. But it's better."

Digital Innovation

CAD-CAM Customised Bone Blocks

The next evolution: sending the patient's CBCT to a human tissue bank, which mills a custom-fit block — reducing placement time from 45 minutes to 45 seconds.

1

CBCT Scan + Digital Planning

Prosthetic position and implant planning defined first. Block shape is designed to support the planned implants with adequate volume.

2

Transmit to Tissue Bank (Austria)

CBCT + digital plan sent to Botiss human tissue bank. Custom block milled from donor bone by robotic CNC process.

3

Block Delivered Sterile

Cleaned, sterilised, patient-specific block mailed to clinic. Available for both horizontal and vertical augmentation.

4

45-Second Placement

Perfectly contoured block seats in defect. Fixed with 2 stainless steel screws. Surgeon's time redirected to flap management — the critical phase.

"What used to take me 45 minutes now takes 45 seconds. I spend most of my time on managing the flap — because 40% of failure is due to wrong flap suturing."

Case Study

Charles, 72 Years Old — CAD-CAM Block

Patient Background

  • 72 years old — previously seen by 5 other implantologists
  • All advised: "go to your chin" or "go to your head"
  • Patient refused conventional autogenous harvesting at his age
  • CBCT showed only 2mm of bone in target area

Surgical Approach

  • Vestibular flap — tension in vestibule, not on crest
  • Decortication performed before CBCT sent to Austria
  • Vertical and horizontal augmentation achieved
  • CAD-CAM block placed in 45 seconds
  • 2 fixation screws + adjacent membrane (pure cancellous)
  • Periosteum, mucosa, muscle all sutured in layers
  • Total procedure: 1 hour including the entire graft

5-Month Follow-Up Results

  • 2mm initial bone → 7.5mm centrally
  • CBCT: nicely integrated augmentation
  • 6 months: two implants placed with GBR, cerabone, Jason membrane
  • Free gingival graft offered (patient declined)

2024 — 5 Year Review (Age 77)

  • Still functioning well with Parkinson's disease
  • 1mm bone loss on one implant — expected long term
  • "I can eat apples for the first time in 20 years"

This patient was comfortable throughout — no black eyes, no morphine, just standard NSAIDs.

Technique

The Sausage Technique

Rather than a rigid block, a non-resorbable membrane creates a tent with bone particles underneath — resembling a sausage shape. Easier, more vascularised, but less volume-stable.

What It Is

  • Non-resorbable or titanium-reinforced membrane used as tent
  • Bone particles (autogenous + xenograft) placed underneath
  • Preferred ratio: 60% autogenous / 40% cerabone for implant sites
  • More space between particles → better vascularisation

Bone Particle Options (Botiss)

  • Autogenous alone (free but limited quantity)
  • MaxGraph — corticocancellous allograft
  • MaxGraph+ — with hyaluronic acid (sticky)
  • Cerabone — xenograft (bovine)
  • Cerabone+ — with hyaluronic acid
  • Mixing cerabone + maxgraph is common practice

Key Disadvantage

Volume loss greater than blocks — particles compress/resorb more. Expect more shrinkage than rigid block augmentation.

Membrane Selection

Choosing the Right Membrane

The membrane choice determines both the containment quality and the healing environment for your bone particles.

Membrane Type Resorbable? Best Used For Main Limitation
Collagen membrane Yes (~12–16 wks) Small defects, autogenous grafts Too short for xenograft timelines
PTFE (e-PTFE) No — removal needed Traditional GBR, large defects Poor bone quality, gelatinous layer
Ti-reinforced PTFE No Vertical augmentation Same as PTFE + mesh removal
Titanium mesh No Large defects, complex 3D High perforation rate
Magnesium (Novamat) Yes — to Mg salts Most GBR scenarios, 2022+ Hydrogen release (puffy but normal)

Current preference: Magnesium membrane for the ability to manage as it resorbs into quality-improving Mg salts. Dense/permanent from Botiss or titanium-reinforced for very large cases.

Traditional GBR

The PTFE Membrane — Old School Problems

For decades, the double-layered PTFE membrane with titanium reinforcement was standard. It works — but at a significant cost.

Known Complications

  • Poor bone quality — drilling feels like drilling through "battery"
  • Exposure = game over — bone quality compromised immediately
  • Gelatinous periosteal layer on removal — body's rejection response
  • Removing gelatinous layer may strip bone if already soft
  • Implant stability unpredictable in poorly-mineralised grafts

The Gelatinous Layer Explained

On PTFE removal, a shiny gelatinous layer is consistently found overlying the bone. This is the body's reaction — a form of separation response.

Attempting to remove it risks removing the bone underneath — especially when that bone is already poorly mineralised.

"The literature tells you the quality of bone under PTFE is going to be very, very weak. The patient was already aware they might need further GBR."

Stage 1 grafting goal: Take the patient from -10 to 0. Ridge contouring at implant placement is a separate, subsequent step.

Surgical Technique

The Khoury Cortical Plate Technique

Named after Lebanese-German surgeon Fouad Khoury, this technique uses a thin cortical plate to create a box, filled with autogenous bone. Later adapted to use ready-made plates from tissue banks.

The Concept

  • 1mm cortical plate harvested from patient's own mouth
  • Fixated with screws to create a box-like shell
  • Middle filled with autogenous cancellous bone
  • Creates a natural barrier and scaffold
  • Tissue bank version: ready-made plates available

Reported Complications

  • Plate must be very thin — thick = rejection
  • Very thin = cracks during fixation
  • Soft tissue dehiscence (plate is devitalized bone)
  • Requires ~8 fixation screws — time consuming
  • Non-integration of the shell
  • Necrotic appearance on occasion
  • Graft exposure and exposed fixation screws

"I tried the cortical plate once. I didn't like it. It's not my cup of tea."

New Paradigm 2022+

Magnesium Membranes — Novamat

How Magnesium Works

  • Resorbable — dissolves into magnesium salts
  • Mg salts strengthen bone quality underneath
  • Produces dense cortical bone (not gelatinous layer)
  • On exposure: Mg + air → alkaline environment
  • Alkaline pH = bacteriostatic effect
  • Improved soft tissue quality even with exposure

Practical Advantages

  • Rigid — curves to shape without cracking
  • Available in multiple sizes
  • Only 2 screws needed (vs 8 for Khoury plate)
  • Can be used as a plate technique (novamat plate)

Post-surgery puffiness from hydrogen gas release is normal and expected — not infection. Resolves within weeks.

Case Study

Japanese Lady — Magnesium Plate Result

Presentation

  • Referred for 4-unit bridge: canine 4, 5, 6
  • Failing canines — not infected, just failing
  • Atrophic ridge at site 5 and 6
  • Knife-edge ridge on CBCT — very thin bone

Surgical Plan

  • Accessed ascending ramus for autogenous bone
  • Large defect required: mixed 50/50 autogenous + MaxGraph
  • Mental nerve window preserved with careful drilling

  • Jason membrane on top layer
  • Periosteal release lingual + suture

Outcome

  • Knife edge → nice wide ridge on CBCT

  • "Perfect circle osteotomy" — unlike marshmallow bone with PTFE
  • 2 implants (5 & 6) + 1 immediate (canine)
  • 4-unit final bridge from Bird Lab

"Contoured magnesium membrane to shape, only 2 screws, filled the defect. Then same story on the other side. She needed another graft and 2 more implants for the 3-unit bridge."

Flap Management

Why Flap Design is Critical

Up to 40% of bone graft failure is attributable to poor flap management and suturing — not the graft material itself.

Common Errors

  • Suturing quickly when tired late in surgery
  • Insufficient periosteal release → tension
  • Inadequate muscle and periosteum layering
  • Too much tension at keratinized/non-keratinized junction
  • Failure to achieve primary closure

Best Practice

  • Vestibular flap — tension in vestibule, not crest
  • Suture: mucosa → periosteum → muscle in layers
  • Spend most time on flap design and closure
  • Use nylon (non-resorbable) — remove at 2–3 weeks
  • Monofilament nylon = less plaque trap than braided

"With CAD-CAM blocks, the bulk of the case is in flap management. The bone part takes 45 seconds."

Evidence Base

Evidence for Staged Bone Grafting

Stage 1 bone grafting is designed to take the site from a deficient baseline (-10) to approximately neutral (0). It is not about perfecting; subsequent ridge contouring handles refinement.

The Stage 1 Goal

Create sufficient bone volume and quality to enable implant placement with primary stability. Ridge contouring at stage 2 and GBR during implant placement are additional separate procedures.

Biomaterial Science

Bone Graft Materials — Making Sense of the Options

By Origin

  • Autogenous — patient's own bone; best biology, two sites
  • Allograft — human donor bone; MaxGraph (Botiss); corticocancellous; good volume + biology
  • Xenograft — animal-derived (bovine = Cerabone); volume stability, slow resorption
  • Alloplast — synthetic; least predictable for large defects

For socket grafting: mix cancellous and cortical particles. Cortical maintains volume; cancellous provides vitality.

Hyaluronic Acid Additions

  • Cerabone+ = Cerabone with hyaluronic acid → sticky, mouldable
  • MaxGraph+ = MaxGraph with hyaluronic acid → can be shaped
  • Hyaluronic acid enables "chewing gum" consistency
  • Easier to handle, less particle scatter

Current Mixing Preference

For large staged augmentations: Cerabone + MaxGraph or autogenous + corticocancellous MaxGraph. No single material is ideal for all cases.

Surgical Instrument

Membrane Stabilisation — Why Pins Matter

A key principle from the course: an unstabilised membrane is an unacceptable membrane. Bone healing requires stability, just as a fractured jaw needs fixation plates.

My Zinger Pin System

  • Grade 5 titanium — extremely solid, can be hammered
  • Flexible carrier: placement at up to 45° angles
  • 3mm depth insertion
  • Polished surface: never integrates to bone; easy removal
  • Comes with 1mm drill for pre-drilling
  • Pins placed on flat surface between roots — avoids PDL

Clinical Precautions

  • Avoid PDL — can cause vitality loss in adjacent teeth
  • Place on broad, flat bone surface — not near roots
  • Thin phenotype: pins may become visible through mucosa
  • Easy removal: 2mm incision + excavator flick
  • No hammering needed with good technique + pre-drill

"A pin that showed through the skin — patient said 'Leave it — it reminds me of the pin you gave me.' You can remove them easily at any time."

Cost Considerations

The Economics of CAD-CAM Augmentation

Costs

  • CAD-CAM block from tissue bank: ~£1,200
  • Allograft block (standard): ~£300
  • Always keep a spare block: extra £300 insurance

"I charge £3,600 for one hour of my time. A £1,200 block for a minimally invasive result is absolutely worth it for the right patient."

Value Proposition

  • Patient avoids black eyes, tramadol, morphine, sick days
  • Surgeon saves 45 minutes of block harvesting
  • Flap management receives full attention
  • Reduced failure risk from fatigue
  • Better patient experience → better reputation

Patient Selection

Not every patient needs or can afford CAD-CAM. Explain the spectrum of options honestly. Willing patients (especially elderly with comorbidities) are ideal candidates.

Clinical Decision Making

When Can We Be Fast? When Must We Be Slow?

😓 Cannot Be Fast

  • No wall remaining (zero-wall defect)
  • Single wall defect only
  • Large vertical + horizontal combined defect
  • Poor patient healing (diabetes, smoking, medications)
  • After failed previous graft — needs longer healing

✓ Can Optimise For Speed

  • 2-3 wall defect (socket grafting with good walls)
  • CAD-CAM block = still staged but surgical time short
  • Good patient biology, non-smoker, no systemic factors
  • Maxilla (better blood supply) heals faster than mandible
  • Horizontal-only defects heal faster than vertical

"Technology allows us to be minimally invasive — but we cannot always be fast. Patients must understand this distinction."

Key Principles

Modern GBR — Core Protocol Summary

🚫

No Cover Screws

Pressure on graft impairs healing. Use healing abutments to tent soft tissue.

📌

Stabilise Membrane

Apical AND coronal pins + sutures. Fibroblast invasion fails if membrane moves.

🏗️

Build Convexity

Creates dead space that guides bone volume and supports mucosa long-term.

🔒

Proper Seal

Hydrophilic membrane + stabilisation = no connective tissue invasion.

These 4 principles apply regardless of which material or membrane you choose. Violate any one of them and the graft outcome is compromised.

Looking Forward

The Future of Bone Augmentation

Implant dentistry moves quickly. In five years we didn't expect magnesium membranes — what comes next will likely be just as transformative.

Current Trends

  • Digital-first planning — CBCT + CAD-CAM default for complex cases
  • Biologically active materials — hyaluronic acid, growth factors
  • Magnesium as standard — replacing PTFE in most scenarios
  • Novamat plate technique — published research emerging

The Balanced Approach

Follow evidence, adopt updates, but don't chase fashions. Ridge preservation was once a huge trend — many mistakes were made. Now we have studies to guide us. The same will happen with every new technique.

"In private practice, are you willing to gamble? If not — stick to GBR. It's safe, predictable, and been there since the mid-80s."

Key Takeaways

1. Intelligent Grafting

Minimally invasive doesn't mean "less good". Modern materials deliver excellent results with less trauma.

2. Evolution, Not Revolution

Autogenous → Allograft → CAD-CAM → Magnesium. Each step improves patient experience without sacrificing outcomes.

3. 40% = Flap Failure

Invest your surgical time in flap design and suturing. CAD-CAM blocks free your hands for what matters.

4. Stabilise Everything

Pins + sutures, apically and coronally. Unstabilised membrane = unacceptable bone graft.

5. Magnesium > PTFE

Resorbable to bone-improving Mg salts. Bacteriostatic on exposure. Dense cortical bone quality outcome.

6. Stage 1 Goal

Take from -10 to 0. Ridge contouring and GBR at implant stage are separate subsequent procedures.
