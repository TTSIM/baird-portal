Foundation

Why Getting Good at Dentures Matters

Mastering dentures builds the foundation for full-mouth rehabilitation and implant dentistry. The skills transfer directly:

  • Midline and incisal plane positioning
  • Occlusal plane management
  • Aesthetic assessment and smile design
  • Transition to full-arch implant work

Masticatory Efficiency

Efficiency drops below 10 functional pairs (5 to 5 dentition). This impacts nutrition, digestion and quality of life.

Patient Pathway

The Rehabilitation Journey

Even seemingly hopeless cases can become lifelong implant patients with the right staged approach.

Key principle: Don't jump straight to implants. Stabilise first, prove the patient can maintain their oral health, then progress to definitive restorations.

Tip 1

Be Holistic

Pay attention to the patient as a whole, not just their teeth. Don't rush into tooth replacement — sometimes the best plan is to wait.

SHORTENED DENTAL ARCH

A 5-to-5 dentition works. Patients can eat all the same foods — it just takes longer to chew. Consider this before adding posterior dentures.

  • Compromised OH? → Optimise first, replace later
  • Only posterior teeth missing? → Short arch may suffice
  • Front teeth missing? → More urgency to replace

DECISION TOOL: Replace now or wait?

  • Patient has good oral hygiene
  • Missing teeth are in the aesthetic zone
  • Patient is motivated to maintain
  • Periodontal disease is controlled
  • Patient has functional concerns

Tips 2 & 3

Sculpt Tissues & Plan for Tooth Loss

Tip 2: Immediate Dentures Shape Tissues

Extend the pontic ~1mm into the extraction socket. This sculpts the soft tissues for a natural ovate emergence when you later do a bridge or implant.

Tip 3: Plan for the Next 5 Years

Ask: "Will this restoration fail in the next 5 years?"

  • If likely → build denture design to allow tooth addition
  • Acrylic → acrylic goes around the tooth
  • Metal → metal framework extends to the tooth
  • Consider replacing with indirect crown + milled rest seats & guide planes

Critical: If doing a metal denture, complete all indirect restorations BEFORE taking your denture impression. Metal cannot be adjusted chairside.

Tip 4

Overdenture Abutments

Retaining teeth as overdenture abutments provides natural socket preservation. The bone stays because the root stays.

  • Grind teeth to gum level
  • Denture sits directly over the top
  • No root canal needed if pulp has receded (e.g. tooth wear)
  • If symptomatic later → extract at that point
  • Soft liner optional — acrylic alone often gives good retention

Lab tip: If using a soft liner, ensure the lab blocks out enough space over retained roots so the liner is thick enough to grip and adhere.

CASE: The Clencher

For heavy clenchers: use wider implants, over-engineer with metal plates, and be aware of increased forces throughout the treatment plan.

Tip 5

Conform or Reorganise?

The single biggest mistake in partial dentures: not deciding this upfront.

Conform (80% of cases)

Keep the patient's existing bite. Straightforward planning.

  • OVD is acceptable facially
  • Reproducible ICP with stable contacts
  • Sufficient space for materials
  • Implant placement is straightforward

Reorganise (20% of cases)

Change the bite. More complex but sometimes necessary.

  • Patient is over-closed
  • No stable ICP
  • Insufficient space for materials
  • Do composites + dentures FIRST, then implants

Golden rule: Composites and acrylic dentures are reversible and adjustable. Establish the new bite with these first. Only then convert to implants and indirect restorations.

Tip 5 continued

Assessing OVD

The Conforming Checklist

  • OVD acceptable facially (say "M", check resting vs biting)
  • Resting VD and occlusal VD within 2–4mm
  • No mentalis muscle activation on closure
  • Reproducible ICP (same position every time)
  • Stable intercuspal contacts present
  • Sufficient space for prosthetic teeth/materials

Tip 6

Acrylic vs Metal vs Valplast

Acrylic ✓

  • Multiple tooth loss likely
  • Stabilisation phase
  • Immediate/interim
  • Testing OVD changes
  • Long spans / few abutments
  • Metallic allergy
  • Cost-effective

Metal (CoCr) ✓

  • Stable dentition
  • No further tooth loss expected (5yr)
  • Long-term prosthesis
  • Better support & retention
  • Thinner, more comfortable
  • Can incorporate rests & clasps

Valplast ✗

  • Engages undercuts → gum stripping
  • Purely mucosal-supported
  • Causes soft tissue & bone loss
  • Discolouration
  • Difficult to polish, repair, adjust
  • Leads to periodontal problems
  • Avoid if implants planned
Tip 7

Survey Your Models

Understanding survey lines is the foundation of good denture design. The surveyor marks the maximum bulbosity (equator) of each tooth.

CLASP SELECTION RULES

Molars → Occlusally approaching

Large circumference allows flexible clasp arm. ⅔ reciprocates, ⅓ retains.

Premolars & Canines → Gingivally approaching

Small circumference makes occlusally approaching clasps too stiff → fracture risk.

Common Undercut Locations

Lower molars: lingual (Curve of Wilson tilt)
Upper molars: mesio-buccal

Don't have a surveyor? Get your lab to survey and send photos. Or invest ~£100 (Geodental).

Tip 7 continued

Guide Planes

Guide planes create a single path of insertion/removal, increasing friction fit and retention.

REQUIREMENTS

  • Minimum 2mm long vertically
  • Must engage slightly into buccal AND palatal aspects
  • Best bur: tungsten carbide shoulder bur (easy to keep parallel)
  • Bilateral saddles: get as parallel as possible on both sides

IN-MOUTH TECHNIQUE

Position patient, retract with mirror, sight both tooth surfaces (e.g. 4 and 7 in a bounded saddle). Hold bur against one tooth, move across to the other, lock your wrist, then cut.

Adjusting Survey Lines

Moving the Survey Line

Survey Line Too Low (Gingival)

Clasp would sit near gingival margin → plaque trap, trauma, perio problems.

Solution: Add flowable composite (e.g. SDR) to the mid-third, distal-buccal aspect. This raises the maximum bulbosity and hides the clasp.

Survey Line Too High (Coronal)

Reciprocating clasp arm sits too high → occlusal interference.

Solution: Use a bur to reduce the bulbosity and move the survey line down the tooth.

Tip 8

Cutting Rest Seats Properly

Rest seats transmit forces down the long axis of the tooth. They're often cut too shallow or too small.

KEY DIMENSIONS

Width at marginal ridge: ½ the bucco-palatal width (~3mm on a molar)

Length (MD): ⅓ to ½ the tooth length (~4–5mm on a molar)

Depth at marginal ridge: 1mm (deepest part)

Depth at centre: 0.5mm (shallowest, follows tooth contour)

With two adjacent teeth: take preparation more lingually for the minor connector. Don't cross the transverse ridge. Always apply fluoride after preparation.

Biomechanics

Reciprocation

Reciprocation prevents orthodontic tooth movement caused by clasps engaging undercuts. Every retentive clasp needs a reciprocating element.

Metal Dentures (CoCr)

  • Occlusally approaching: ⅔ of clasp arm above survey line provides reciprocation
  • Three-arm clasps: one side reciprocates, one side retains
  • Rests on opposing surfaces of the tooth

Acrylic Dentures

  • Stainless steel clasps deform easily → quickly lose reciprocation
  • MUST have acrylic plate sitting above the survey line on the opposing side
  • Never rely on the other clasp arm for reciprocation

Tips 9 & 10

Free-End Saddles: The Special Case

Free-end saddles need special attention because the mucosa posteriorly is compressible (spongy), while the teeth anteriorly are rigid.

When biting, the denture sinks posteriorly. Clasps lift off the anterior teeth. Indirect retention is essential to counteract this.

TISSUE STOPS: The Game Changer

Tissue stops are blocks of tray material on non-compressible tissue (ridge crest, hard palate). They create a tripod so the tray sits at one precise level.

  • Even impression material thickness throughout
  • Same tray position for border moulding and impression
  • Can check tray extension accurately
  • Free — lab just cuts holes in the spacer

Without tissue stops: You push the unspaced tray to the ridge → can't check for overextension → inaccurate impression.

Tip 11

Border Moulding with Pink Stick

Treat free-end saddle areas like a complete denture — border mould them.

Green Stick (Traditional)

Messy, cools quickly, doesn't flow well. Needs a Bunsen burner or hot air blower. Apply small increments along the tray border, manipulate cheeks/lips.

Pink Stick (Recommended) ✓

Lower melting temperature. Warm in hot water (~45°C). Place in the centre of the tray over the ridge crest, seat tray until tissue stops touch. It flows buccally and lingually. Patient swallows, moves tongue L/R — done.

STEP-BY-STEP PROTOCOL

1. Check tray extensions ▾

Trim if overextended. Tray should be 2–3mm short of the sulcus depth.

2. Warm pink stick in hot water ▾

~45°C from the tap is fine. Much easier than heating green stick over a flame.

3. Place on crest of ridge ▾

Only on the free-end saddle area. Alginate handles other areas.

4. Seat until tissue stops touch ▾

Push down until you can't go further. Pink stick flows B/L automatically.

5. Functional movements ▾

Patient swallows, moves tongue left and right. Pink stick records the functional border.

6. Take impression with alginate ▾

Border moulding is preserved. Alginate fills the remaining space for the secondary impression.

Tip 12

Shimstock Foil: Track Your Bite

Shimstock foil is ~8 microns thick. If it holds between teeth when the patient bites, you know within 8μm that there's contact. Use it at every stage.

COMMON MISTAKE

Putting too much silicone bite registration material between the blocks. This props open the casts beyond the true bite. Only place silicone where teeth are MISSING, not all the way around.

Summary

The 13 Tips at a Glance

1. Be holistic — treat the patient, not just the teeth

2. Use immediate dentures to sculpt tissues

3. Plan for tooth loss in the next 5 years

4. Consider overdenture abutments

5. Decide: conform or reorganise

6. Choose acrylic vs metal wisely (avoid Valplast)

7. Get models surveyed + cut guide planes

8. Cut rest seats properly (1mm deep at ridge)

9. Always consider indirect retention

10. Use tissue stops on special trays

11. Border mould free-end saddles (pink stick)

12. Use shimstock foil at every stage

13. Don't guess — use pressure indicators for adjustments
